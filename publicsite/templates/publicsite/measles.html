Measles (Rubeola)

Overview

Measles is a highly contagious, serious viral disease that remains a leading cause of death among young children globally, despite the availability of a safe and effective vaccine. Measles is caused by a virus in the paramyxovirus family and is normally passed through direct contact and through the air.

The measles vaccine has been in use since the 1960s. It is safe, effective, and inexpensive. WHO recommends immunization for all susceptible children and adults for whom measles vaccination is not contraindicated. Two doses of the measles vaccine are about 97% effective at preventing measles.

Before the introduction of the measles vaccine in 1963, major epidemics occurred approximately every 2-3 years, causing an estimated 2.6 million deaths each year. Despite significant progress, measles continues to cause outbreaks worldwide, particularly in areas with low vaccination coverage.

Symptoms of Measles

Symptoms typically appear 7-14 days after exposure to the virus. Measles is contagious from 4 days before to 4 days after the rash appears.

Early Symptoms

High fever (often >104°F/40°C), cough, runny nose, red watery eyes (conjunctivitis), and fatigue

Koplik Spots

Small white spots with bluish-white centers inside the mouth, appearing 2-3 days before the rash

Measles Rash

Red, blotchy rash that starts on the face and behind the ears, then spreads downward to the chest, arms, and legs

Fever Pattern

Fever rises as rash appears, reaching up to 104-105°F, then subsides as rash fades

Contagious Period

From 4 days before rash appears until 4 days after rash appears

Duration

Illness typically lasts 7-10 days, with rash fading in the same order it appeared

Prevention & Vaccination

MMR Vaccine

Primary Prevention

Two doses: First at 12-15 months, second at 4-6 years

97% effective against measles

MMRV Vaccine

Combination Vaccine

Protects against measles, mumps, rubella, and varicella

Available for children 12 months to 12 years

Outbreak Response

Mass Vaccination

During outbreaks, vaccination campaigns target all susceptible individuals

Post-exposure prophylaxis within 72 hours

Travel Precautions

International Travel

Infants 6-11 months need early MMR dose before international travel

Check vaccination status before travel

Complications of Measles

Measles can cause serious, sometimes fatal complications, especially in children under 5 years, adults over 20, pregnant women, and immunocompromised individuals.

Pneumonia

The most common cause of death from measles. Accounts for 60% of measles-related deaths. Can be viral or secondary bacterial pneumonia.

Encephalitis

Brain swelling occurring in 1 in 1,000 cases. Can cause permanent brain damage, seizures, intellectual disability, or death.

SSPE

Subacute sclerosing panencephalitis - a fatal brain disease that develops 7-10 years after measles infection. 100% fatal.

Ear Infections

Occurs in 1 in 10 children with measles. Can lead to permanent hearing loss.

Severe Diarrhea

Common complication that can lead to dehydration and malnutrition, especially in developing countries.

Pregnancy Risks

Increased risk of miscarriage, preterm birth, and low birth weight. No congenital syndrome but serious for mother.

WHO Global Impact Data:

Before widespread vaccination, measles caused an estimated 2.6 million deaths annually. Despite a 73% reduction in measles deaths between 2000-2018, measles still caused over 140,000 deaths in 2018, mostly children under 5. Measles remains endemic in many parts of the world.
