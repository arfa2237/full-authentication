{% trans "Measles (Rubeola)" %}

{% trans "Overview" %}

{% trans "Measles is a highly contagious, serious viral disease that remains a leading cause of death among young children globally, despite the availability of a safe and effective vaccine. Measles is caused by a virus in the paramyxovirus family and is normally passed through direct contact and through the air." %}

{% trans "The measles vaccine has been in use since the 1960s. It is safe, effective, and inexpensive. WHO recommends immunization for all susceptible children and adults for whom measles vaccination is not contraindicated. Two doses of the measles vaccine are about 97% effective at preventing measles." %}

{% trans "Before the introduction of the measles vaccine in 1963, major epidemics occurred approximately every 2-3 years, causing an estimated 2.6 million deaths each year. Despite significant progress, measles continues to cause outbreaks worldwide, particularly in areas with low vaccination coverage." %}

{% trans "Symptoms of Measles" %}

{% trans "Symptoms typically appear 7-14 days after exposure to the virus." %} {% trans "Measles is contagious from 4 days before to 4 days after the rash appears." %}

{% trans "Early Symptoms" %}

{% trans "High fever (often >104°F/40°C), cough, runny nose, red watery eyes (conjunctivitis), and fatigue" %}

{% trans "Koplik Spots" %}

{% trans "Small white spots with bluish-white centers inside the mouth, appearing 2-3 days before the rash" %}

{% trans "Measles Rash" %}

{% trans "Red, blotchy rash that starts on the face and behind the ears, then spreads downward to the chest, arms, and legs" %}

{% trans "Fever Pattern" %}

{% trans "Fever rises as rash appears, reaching up to 104-105°F, then subsides as rash fades" %}

{% trans "Contagious Period" %}

{% trans "From 4 days before rash appears until 4 days after rash appears" %}

{% trans "Duration" %}

{% trans "Illness typically lasts 7-10 days, with rash fading in the same order it appeared" %}

{% trans "Complications of Measles" %}

{% trans "Measles can cause serious, sometimes fatal complications," %} {% trans "especially in children under 5 years, adults over 20, pregnant women, and immunocompromised individuals." %}

{% trans "Pneumonia" %}

{% trans "The most common cause of death from measles. Accounts for 60% of measles-related deaths. Can be viral or secondary bacterial pneumonia." %}

{% trans "Encephalitis" %}

{% trans "Brain swelling occurring in 1 in 1,000 cases. Can cause permanent brain damage, seizures, intellectual disability, or death." %}

{% trans "SSPE" %}

{% trans "Subacute sclerosing panencephalitis - a fatal brain disease that develops 7-10 years after measles infection. 100% fatal." %}

{% trans "Ear Infections" %}

{% trans "Occurs in 1 in 10 children with measles. Can lead to permanent hearing loss." %}

{% trans "Severe Diarrhea" %}

{% trans "Common complication that can lead to dehydration and malnutrition, especially in developing countries." %}

{% trans "Pregnancy Risks" %}

{% trans "Increased risk of miscarriage, preterm birth, and low birth weight. No congenital syndrome but serious for mother." %}

{% trans "WHO Global Impact Data:" %}

{% trans "Before widespread vaccination, measles caused an estimated 2.6 million deaths annually. Despite a 73% reduction in measles deaths between 2000-2018, measles still caused over 140,000 deaths in 2018, mostly children under 5. Measles remains endemic in many parts of the world." %}
