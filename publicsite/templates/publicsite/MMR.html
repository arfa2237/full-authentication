Measles, Mumps & Rubella (MMR)

Overview

Measles, mumps, and rubella are three serious viral diseases that can lead to severe complications, permanent disabilities, and death. These diseases are highly contagious and spread through respiratory droplets, but can be effectively prevented by the MMR vaccine.

The MMR vaccine provides protection against all three diseases in a single shot. According to WHO, two doses of the MMR vaccine are about 97% effective at preventing measles, 88% effective at preventing mumps, and 97% effective at preventing rubella.

Despite being vaccine-preventable, these diseases continue to cause outbreaks worldwide, particularly in areas with low vaccination coverage. WHO recommends routine MMR vaccination for all children, with the first dose at 12-15 months and the second dose at 4-6 years.

Symptoms of MMR Diseases

Each disease has distinct symptoms, but all can be prevented by the MMR vaccine. Symptoms typically appear 7-14 days after exposure for measles, 12-25 days for mumps, and 14-21 days for rubella.

Measles Symptoms

High fever, cough, runny nose, red watery eyes, Koplik spots (white spots in mouth), and characteristic red rash that spreads from face downward

Mumps Symptoms

Fever, headache, muscle aches, tiredness, loss of appetite, and painful swelling of salivary glands (parotitis) under ears or jaw

Rubella Symptoms

Mild fever, sore throat, rash starting on face and spreading, swollen lymph nodes, and red itchy eyes. Often mild in children

Common Early Symptoms

Fever, fatigue, loss of appetite, and general malaise (feeling unwell) before specific symptoms appear

Contagious Period

Measles: 4 days before to 4 days after rash appears. Mumps: 2 days before to 5 days after swelling. Rubella: 1 week before to 1 week after rash

Duration

Measles: 7-10 days. Mumps: 7-10 days. Rubella: 2-3 days. Complete recovery may take longer

Prevention & Vaccination

MMR Vaccine

Primary Prevention

Two doses: First at 12-15 months, second at 4-6 years

97% effective against measles and rubella

MMRV Vaccine

Combination Vaccine

Protects against measles, mumps, rubella, and varicella (chickenpox)

Available for children 12 months to 12 years

Adult Vaccination

Catch-up Doses

Adults born after 1957 should have at least one MMR dose

Healthcare workers need two doses

Contraindications

Important Exceptions

Not for pregnant women, immunocompromised, or severe allergy to vaccine components

Consult healthcare provider

Complications of MMR Diseases

These diseases can cause serious, sometimes fatal complications, especially in young children, pregnant women, and immunocompromised individuals. Vaccination prevents these complications.

Measles Complications

Pneumonia (most common cause of death), encephalitis (brain swelling), severe diarrhea, ear infections leading to permanent hearing loss, and subacute sclerosing panencephalitis (SSPE) - fatal brain disease years later

Mumps Complications

Orchitis (testicular inflammation) in post-pubertal males, oophoritis (ovarian inflammation), pancreatitis, meningitis, encephalitis, and permanent hearing loss (usually unilateral)

Rubella Complications

Congenital Rubella Syndrome (CRS) in babies born to infected mothers - causes deafness, cataracts, heart defects, intellectual disabilities. Arthritis in women, thrombocytopenic purpura, encephalitis

Pregnancy Risks

Measles: Increased risk of miscarriage, preterm birth, low birth weight. Rubella: Up to 90% risk of CRS if infected in first trimester. Mumps: Increased risk of fetal loss in first trimester

Long-term Effects

Permanent disabilities including blindness, deafness, brain damage, heart defects, and in case of SSPE from measles - inevitable death years after initial infection

Death Rates

Measles: 1-3 per 1000 cases in developed countries, up to 10% in developing regions. Mumps: Case-fatality rate 1.6-3.8 per 10,000. Rubella: Rare except in CRS cases

WHO Global Impact Data:

Before widespread vaccination, measles caused an estimated 2.6 million deaths annually. Despite a 73% reduction in measles deaths between 2000-2018, measles still caused over 140,000 deaths in 2018, mostly children under 5. Rubella causes approximately 100,000 cases of Congenital Rubella Syndrome annually worldwide.
