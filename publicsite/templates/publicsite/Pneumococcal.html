{% trans "Pneumococcal Disease" %}

{% trans "Overview" %}

{% trans "Pneumococcal disease is caused by the bacterium Streptococcus pneumoniae, which can lead to serious infections in the lungs (pneumonia), bloodstream (bacteremia), and brain/spinal cord (meningitis)." %}

{% trans "Pneumococcal bacteria are common inhabitants of the upper respiratory tract and can be spread through respiratory droplets from coughing or sneezing. While many people carry the bacteria without symptoms, it can cause severe illness in vulnerable populations." %}

{% trans "The pneumococcal vaccine is highly effective in preventing the most serious forms of pneumococcal disease. WHO recommends pneumococcal conjugate vaccines (PCV) for all children and high-risk adults." %}

{% trans "Pneumococcal Disease Symptoms" %}

{% trans "Symptoms vary depending on the type of infection." %} {% trans "Pneumococcal disease can manifest as pneumonia, meningitis, or bacteremia, each with distinct symptoms." %}

{% trans "Pneumonia Symptoms" %}

{% trans "Fever, chills, cough, rapid breathing, chest pain, and difficulty breathing" %}

{% trans "Meningitis Symptoms" %}

{% trans "Stiff neck, fever, headache, nausea, vomiting, sensitivity to light, confusion" %}

{% trans "Bacteremia Symptoms" %}

{% trans "Fever, chills, low alertness, abdominal pain, joint pain in children" %}

{% trans "Middle Ear Infection" %}

{% trans "Ear pain, red or swollen eardrum, fever, sleep disturbances in children" %}

{% trans "Sinus Infections" %}

{% trans "Headache, stuffy or runny nose, loss of smell, facial pain or pressure" %}

{% trans "General Symptoms" %}

{% trans "Fatigue, loss of appetite, irritability (especially in young children)" %}

{% trans "If Pneumococcal Vaccines Are Not Taken" %}

{% trans "Pneumococcal disease remains a leading cause of vaccine-preventable deaths worldwide." %} {% trans "Without vaccination, vulnerable populations face severe complications." %}

{% trans "Pneumonia Complications" %}

{% trans "Respiratory failure, lung abscesses, pleural effusion (fluid around lungs), and sepsis" %}

{% trans "Meningitis Damage" %}

{% trans "Brain damage, hearing loss, learning disabilities, seizures, and permanent neurological damage" %}

{% trans "Bacteremia Outcomes" %}

{% trans "Septic shock, organ failure, tissue damage, and potential limb amputation in severe cases" %}

{% trans "Long-term Disabilities" %}

{% trans "Developmental delays in children, cognitive impairment, and permanent physical disabilities" %}

{% trans "Hearing & Vision Loss" %}

{% trans "Permanent hearing impairment and vision problems due to nerve damage from infection" %}

{% trans "Death" %}

{% trans "Mortality rates up to 20% for pneumococcal meningitis and 5-7% for bacteremic pneumonia" %}

{% trans "WHO Impact Data:" %}

{% trans "According to WHO, pneumococcal pneumonia kills approximately 826,000 children under 5 years annually, making it the leading vaccine-preventable cause of childhood mortality. Vaccination has reduced invasive pneumococcal disease by up to 80% in vaccinated populations." %}
