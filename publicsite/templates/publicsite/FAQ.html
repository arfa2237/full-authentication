Vaccines are biological preparations that provide active acquired immunity to specific infectious diseases. They contain agents that resemble disease-causing microorganisms and are often made from weakened or killed forms of the microbe.

How they work: When administered, vaccines stimulate the body's immune system to recognize the agent as a threat, destroy it, and remember it. This creates immunological memory that allows the immune system to more easily recognize and destroy any of these microorganisms that it later encounters.

WHO Reference: Vaccines train your immune system to create antibodies, just as it does when exposed to a disease. However, because vaccines contain only killed or weakened forms of germs, they do not cause the disease or put you at risk of its complications.

Vaccination is one of the most effective ways to prevent diseases and save lives. Here's why it's crucial:

  • Disease Prevention: Prevents 3.5-5 million deaths annually from diseases like diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, influenza and measles
  • Herd Immunity: Protects vulnerable populations who cannot be vaccinated (newborns, elderly, immunocompromised)
  • Disease Eradication: Has successfully eradicated smallpox and nearly eliminated polio worldwide
  • Cost-Effective: Saves healthcare costs by preventing expensive treatments and hospitalizations
  • Future Protection: Provides long-term immunity and protects future generations

During the first year, your child should receive these essential vaccines according to the EPI schedule:

  • At Birth: BCG (Tuberculosis), OPV-0 (Polio), Hepatitis B-1
  • 6 Weeks: Penta-1 (5 diseases), OPV-1, PCV-1, Rota-1
  • 10 Weeks: Penta-2, OPV-2, PCV-2, Rota-2
  • 14 Weeks: Penta-3, OPV-3, PCV-3
  • 9 Months: Measles-1
WHO Recommendation: Complete immunization before age 2 provides protection during the most vulnerable period of childhood. Never delay essential vaccines.

If your child misses a scheduled vaccine dose, follow these important steps:

Important Rule: DO NOT restart the vaccine series. Continue from where you left off.

  1. Contact Immediately: Reach out to your healthcare provider or vaccination center
  2. Schedule Catch-up: Book the missed dose as soon as possible
  3. Continue Schedule: Complete the remaining doses on the regular schedule
  4. Update Records: Ensure digital records are updated with the catch-up dose
  5. Set Reminders: Use our platform to set reminders for future appointments

Most vaccines can be administered later than recommended without losing effectiveness.

Yes, vaccines undergo the most rigorous testing of any medical product. The safety evaluation process includes:

  • Pre-clinical Testing: Laboratory and animal studies (2-4 years)
  • Phase 1 Trials: Small group safety testing (20-100 people)
  • Phase 2 Trials: Expanded safety and dosage studies (several hundred people)
  • Phase 3 Trials: Large-scale efficacy and safety (thousands of people)
  • Regulatory Review: Independent evaluation by health authorities
  • Post-Marketing Surveillance: Continuous monitoring after approval

This process typically takes 10-15 years before a vaccine is approved for public use.

Most vaccine side effects are mild and temporary, indicating that the body is building protection:

Common Mild Reactions: These usually last 1-2 days and include soreness at injection site, mild fever, fatigue, fussiness (in children), and loss of appetite.

Typical Side Effects by Age:

  • Infants: Sleepiness, temporary appetite loss, mild fever
  • Children: Sore arm, low-grade fever, mild rash
  • Adults: Injection site redness/swelling, headache, muscle aches

Note: Serious side effects are extremely rare (less than 1 in a million doses). The benefits of vaccination far outweigh the minimal risks.

Herd immunity occurs when a high percentage of a community is immune to a disease, making its spread unlikely. This protects vulnerable individuals who cannot be vaccinated.

Importance:

  • Protects newborns who are too young for vaccination
  • Shields elderly with weakened immune systems
  • Safeguards people with medical conditions preventing vaccination
  • Helps eventually eliminate diseases from communities

Thresholds needed for herd immunity:

  • Measles: 95% vaccination rate
  • Polio: 80-85% vaccination rate
  • Diphtheria: 85% vaccination rate
  • COVID-19: 70-90% depending on variants

Yes, receiving multiple vaccines during a single visit is both safe and recommended. Here's why:

  • Immune Capacity: A child's immune system can handle thousands of antigens simultaneously. Even with multiple vaccines, we use only a tiny fraction of immune capacity.
  • Schedule Adherence: Giving multiple vaccines together ensures your child stays up-to-date with the immunization schedule
  • Fewer Visits: Reduces the number of clinic visits, saving time and reducing stress for children and parents
  • Same Efficacy: Each vaccine works as effectively when given alone or in combination
WHO Position: Scientific data show that administering several vaccines at the same time has no adverse effect on a child's immune system. Combination vaccines reduce the number of injections and clinic visits.
