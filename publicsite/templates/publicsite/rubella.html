{% trans "Rubella (German Measles)" %}

{% trans "Overview" %}

{% trans "Rubella, also known as German measles or three-day measles, is a contagious viral infection caused by the rubella virus. While rubella is generally mild in children and adults, it poses a serious threat to pregnant women and their developing babies." %}

{% trans "The rubella vaccine has been available since 1969. The MMR vaccine provides about 97% protection against rubella after two doses. The primary goal of rubella vaccination is to prevent Congenital Rubella Syndrome (CRS), which can cause severe birth defects." %}

{% trans "Rubella was a common childhood disease before the vaccine. Since vaccination programs began, rubella and CRS have been eliminated from many developed countries. WHO has set a goal to eliminate rubella in at least five WHO regions by 2025." %}

{% trans "Symptoms of Rubella" %}

{% trans "Symptoms typically appear 14-21 days after exposure to the virus." %} {% trans "Up to 50% of rubella infections are subclinical (no symptoms)." %}

{% trans "Early Symptoms" %}

{% trans "Low-grade fever (below 101°F/38.3°C), headache, runny nose, red eyes, and general malaise" %}

{% trans "Lymphadenopathy" %}

{% trans "Swollen, tender lymph nodes behind the ears and at the back of the neck (characteristic sign)" %}

{% trans "Rubella Rash" %}

{% trans "Pink or light red rash that starts on the face and spreads to the trunk, arms, and legs. Fades in 2-3 days" %}

{% trans "Joint Pain" %}

{% trans "Arthritis or arthralgia (joint pain) common in adult women, affecting fingers, wrists, and knees" %}

{% trans "Contagious Period" %}

{% trans "From 1 week before to 1 week after rash appears. Most contagious when rash is developing" %}

{% trans "Duration" %}

{% trans "Illness typically lasts 2-3 days, which is why it's called 'three-day measles'" %}

{% trans "Prevention & Vaccination" %}

{% trans "MMR Vaccine" %}

{% trans "Primary Prevention" %}

{% trans "Two doses: First at 12-15 months, second at 4-6 years" %}

{% trans "97% effective against rubella" %}

{% trans "Preconception Vaccination" %}

{% trans "For Women" %}

{% trans "Women should be vaccinated at least 1 month before becoming pregnant" %}

{% trans "No pregnancy for 4 weeks after vaccination" %}

{% trans "CRS Elimination" %}

{% trans "Global Goal" %}

{% trans "WHO aims to eliminate rubella and CRS in 5 regions by 2025" %}

{% trans "Through high vaccination coverage" %}

{% trans "Rubella Screening" %}

{% trans "Prenatal Care" %}

{% trans "Pregnant women tested for rubella immunity early in pregnancy" %}

{% trans "Non-immune women vaccinated after delivery" %}

{% trans "Complications of Rubella" %}

{% trans "The most serious complication of rubella is Congenital Rubella Syndrome (CRS)" %} {% trans "in babies born to infected mothers, especially during the first trimester of pregnancy." %}

{% trans "Congenital Rubella Syndrome" %}

{% trans "Devastating condition causing multiple birth defects including deafness, cataracts, heart defects, and intellectual disabilities" %}

{% trans "CRS - Hearing Loss" %}

{% trans "Sensorineural hearing loss affects up to 80% of CRS cases. Can be unilateral or bilateral." %}

{% trans "CRS - Eye Defects" %}

{% trans "Cataracts (cloudy lens), glaucoma, retinopathy, and microphthalmia (abnormally small eyes)" %}

{% trans "CRS - Heart Defects" %}

{% trans "Pulmonary artery stenosis, patent ductus arteriosus, and other congenital heart abnormalities" %}

{% trans "Arthritis" %}

{% trans "Joint pain and swelling occurs in up to 70% of adult women with rubella. Usually resolves but can be chronic" %}

{% trans "Thrombocytopenia" %}

{% trans "Low platelet count causing bleeding tendency. Usually resolves but may require treatment" %}

{% trans "WHO Global Impact Data:" %}

{% trans "Rubella causes approximately 100,000 cases of Congenital Rubella Syndrome annually worldwide. Since rubella vaccine introduction, rubella and CRS have been eliminated from the Americas and many other regions. WHO has set a goal to eliminate rubella in at least five WHO regions by 2025 through high vaccination coverage." %}
